Animal, vegetable or mineral?

Facts checked 4 November 2026 by opening the sources listed below. English only.

Please note: this is a short history note, for interest. BrainPlay is for entertainment and practice. It has not been shown to prevent or treat dementia and is not a medical assessment.

Twenty Questions is an old parlor game: one person thinks of something, and the others try to work out what it is by asking questions that can be answered yes or no, twenty at most.

On the radio

A radio version, Twenty Questions, was first broadcast on February 2, 1946, on the Mutual Broadcasting System, from New York. Bill Slater was the moderator, and he opened each round by saying whether the subject was animal, vegetable or mineral.1,2

The show was created by Fred Van Deventer, a newscaster at the station WOR, who sat on the panel with his wife, Florence Rinard, and their son; their daughter Nancy sometimes joined them. Listeners sent in the subjects for the panel to guess.1 A 1946 Time magazine piece credits Nancy, then 16, with suggesting the animal, vegetable or mineral opening.3 The last radio show was broadcast in March 1954.1

Our version

In BrainPlay's Twenty Questions, the computer picks one of thirty everyday things, and you choose questions from a list. Every thing in the list has its own pattern of answers, so the questions can always pin it down. A good question splits what is left roughly in half. There is no clock.

In short: a game that needs nothing but questions moved from the parlor to the radio, and it still works the same way.
