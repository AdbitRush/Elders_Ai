Painting by numbers: a 1950s pastime

Facts checked 4 November 2026 by opening the sources listed below. English only.

Please note: this is a short history note, for interest. BrainPlay is for entertainment and practice. It has not been shown to prevent or treat dementia and is not a medical assessment.

Paint-by-number kits gave people a canvas printed with outlined areas, each marked with a number, and little pots of paint numbered to match. Fill every area with its paint and a finished picture appears.

Where the kits came from

Obituaries in 2019 describe Dan Robbins as a package designer at the Palmer Paint Company in Detroit. The company's owner, Max Klein, wanted new ways to sell more paint, and Robbins came up with the numbered-outline idea.1,2

The kits were sold under the name Craft Master. The Detroit Historical Society says Robbins had the idea in 1949 and dates the first six Craft Master kits to 1951. Sales were slow at first.2

A craze, and a museum show

By the mid-1950s the kits sold in the millions, though the sources we found give the totals differently, so we do not repeat a single figure here.1,2 In 2001 the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History put on an exhibition about them.2 Robbins died on April 1, 2019, at the age of 93.1

Our version

BrainPlay's Color by Number uses the same numbered-area idea on small original pixel pictures made for the site. It is a calm, unhurried pastime: there is no clock and no way to lose. It is meant for enjoyment, not as a test.

In short: numbered outlines turned painting into a pastime anyone could finish. Our game borrows only the idea; the pictures are our own.
