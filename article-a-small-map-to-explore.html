A small map to explore

Evidence checked 3 November 2026. English only.

Please note: BrainPlay is for entertainment and practice. It has not been shown to prevent or treat dementia and is not a medical assessment.

A library beside a bakery. A fountain before a left turn. A map puzzle can invite you to notice how places fit together, then try a route. You can enjoy that challenge without treating it as a test of whether you can find your way in real life.

What navigation research found

A study reported that healthy younger and older men who practised a demanding virtual navigation task every other day over four months improved on navigation performance. Their hippocampal volumes remained stable during the training and at a later follow-up, whereas control groups showed decreases. We reviewed the published abstract, not the full original paper. The all-male sample and sustained programme matter. This was not a trial of a short BrainPlay map game. [1]

A 2022 scoping review examined ten navigation-training studies. Designs, activities and measures varied. Six studies reported improvement in at least one spatial outcome, but the review explicitly said the evidence did not allow conclusions about transfer to everyday navigation. [2] A laboratory result is not a promise about an unfamiliar street, driving or independent travel.

Keep the exploration inside the puzzle

A friendly map game can begin with three landmarks and an always-available map. Try a route with arrow buttons, check where it leads, then change it. A later optional mode could hide the map briefly, with a reminder button when you want it.

Seeing the map again is assistance, not failure. The puzzle should let you finish after seeing the solution rather than require repeated perfect attempts. The route length and number of turns are game settings, not a validated training dose.

There is no need to deliberately get lost, put away navigation aids or take an unfamiliar real-world route for the sake of a game. Keep ordinary travel supports available.

The bottom line: A map can make an interesting puzzle. Research on trained spatial tasks does not establish that BrainPlay improves everyday wayfinding or protects brain health.
